Novel Interaction with Visualizations

For this lab, I extended my Lab 7 temporal commercial network with hands-free voice interaction. After explicitly starting the microphone, you can navigate the 60-day visualization, control playback, and highlight a company by speaking a command. Visible feedback reports what the system heard and which visualization action it performed. Every original mouse, keyboard, button, slider, dragging, and tooltip interaction remains available as a conventional fallback.

2. Voice Interaction

Speak a Visualization Command

Select Start Voice Control, approve microphone access if your browser asks, and speak one command in English. Listening ends after that attempt or after 20 seconds; you can also select Cancel Listening. The microphone is never activated automatically. Watch the feedback cards for the recognition state, transcript, and resulting action. If speech recognition is unsupported or unsuccessful, use the complete conventional controls in Section 3.

5. Design Reflection

Why Voice Supports Temporal Exploration

I extended Lab 7's temporal commercial network with browser-based voice commands for playback, day selection, and company highlighting. The task is to compare transaction activity across 60 days and inspect a company's relationships. “Go to day thirty” displays that day's network, “Pause” holds the frame for inspection, and “Highlight Ion Systems” emphasizes the company and its active links. Stable positions help users track changes in node size and connectivity. Voice suits these discrete actions because users can name a day or company while watching the network, rather than locating controls. Dragging remains preferable for precise spatial adjustments.

Testing also exposed limitations. Automated transcript tests initially rejected “Go to day thirty” because the parser accepted digits only. This was a command-parsing limitation, now corrected. A live Safari attempt encountered denied permission, while a subsequent user test successfully reached Day 30. That success does not establish reliability across accents or noisy environments. The displayed transcript and action let users check the interpretation; buttons, the slider, and mouse/keyboard inspection preserve access when speech fails. Listening requires explicit activation and can be cancelled.
