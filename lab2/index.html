Multivariate Visualization with D3

This multivariate scatterplot compares population, average temperature, development level, and region for twelve cities.

X-axis Population (millions) Y-axis Temperature (°C) Symbol size Development level (Low/Medium/High) Color and shape Region (N/S/E/W)

Move your mouse over each icon to see detailed information of each city!

Design Notes

Design Justification

Upon viewing the dataset, I noticed an apparent relationship between average temperature and population, so I created a scatterplot that allows viewers to explore that relationship while also seeing development level and region. I mapped population to the x-axis and average temperature to the y-axis because position along shared quantitative scales supports clear comparisons between cities. Development level is represented by symbol size: Low, Medium, and High appear as progressively larger marks, which communicates the category order intuitively. Region is represented by both color and shape. I chose this combination because shape provides another way to distinguish regions when color differences are difficult to perceive. It also makes the four regional groups clearer than color alone. Direct labels identify the cities, and the interactive tooltips provide their exact values. Together, these encodings let viewers examine the relationship between population and temperature, compare development levels, and look for regional clusters or exceptions across all twelve cities.
