Interactive Network Visualization with D3

For this lab, I first implemented the two visualizations, the Node Link Visualization and the Adjacency Matrix, respectively, followed by relevant descriptions and my answers to the questions. Enjoy!

1. Dataset Description

View 1

2. Node Link Visualization

Feel free to drag any station to rearrange the network visualization. You can also hover over or focus a station to highlight its direct neighbors. You can also inspect the stations and routes through their tooltips.

3. Node-Link Design Description

In the node-link view, color identifies district and circle area represents daily passengers on a common scale. Fill distinguishes station roles: solid for Terminal, half-filled for Transfer, and outlined for Local. Solid, dashed, and dotted edges represent Express, Metro, and Shuttle, while width represents travel time. Dragging separates overlaps, highlighting isolates neighbors, and tooltips give exact values.

View 2

4. Adjacency Matrix

For the Adjacency Matrix, we use the rows and columns to represent the same district-grouped station order. Notice that each colored cell marks a direct connection. Color denotes route type, while density denotes travel time. Enjoy!

5. Matrix Design Description

The matrix places the same district-grouped order on both axes, so a filled cell identifies a direct connection. Red, green, and blue distinguish Express, Metro, and Shuttle; three density levels classify travel time. Pink axis strips retain district membership. Grouping reveals within- and cross-district patterns.

6. Network Questions and Findings

  1. Which stations appear central in the network?

    This question can be answered partially with the node-link view. Hover highlighting exposes each immediate neighborhood. Fourteen stations share the maximum degree of three, so the visualization does not identify one uniquely central hub.

  2. Which districts are strongly connected to one another?

    This question is answered well by the adjacency matrix. The grouped label strips and filled cells reveal 11 East–South connections, compared with nine Central–North and nine East–West connections.

  3. Where are transfer or terminal stations located in the topology?

    This question is answered well by the node-link view. The half-filled and solid circles show Transfer and Terminal stations distributed along the main chain and at shortcut endpoints rather than concentrated in one cluster.

  4. Which stations have high passenger volume?

    This question is answered well by the node-link view. Circle area makes Stations 46–50 visibly largest, and Station 50 is the leading station with 9,850 daily passengers.

  5. Where are the longest direct travel-time connections?

    This question is answered well by both views. The widest edges and darkest matrix cells identify Stations 7–31 at 16 minutes, 18–34 at 15 minutes, and 29–50 at 14 minutes.

  6. Are particular route types concentrated in particular parts of the network?

    This question is answered partially by the adjacency matrix. Cell colors show broad dispersal: Metro and Shuttle each have four East–South links, while Express has four links in both Central–North and East–West.
