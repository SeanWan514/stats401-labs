Interactive Network Visualization with D3

For this lab, I first implemented the two visualizations, the Node Link Visualization and the Adjacency Matrix, respectively, followed by relevant descriptions and my answers to the questions. Enjoy!

1. Dataset Description

Network Urban Transit Network, using the dataset provided Nodes 50 stations Edges/Links 50 connections/edges Direction Undirected

View 1

2. Node Link Visualization

Feel free to drag any station to rearrange the network visualization. You can also hover over or focus a station to highlight its direct neighbors. You can also inspect the stations and routes through their tooltips.

3. Node-Link Design Description

District color identifies location; circle area represents daily passengers. Fill distinguishes roles: solid means Terminal, half-filled means Transfer, and outlined means Local. Solid, dashed, and dotted links identify Express, Metro, and Shuttle; width represents travel time. Dragging reduces overlap, highlighting isolates neighbors, and tooltips give exact values.

View 2

4. Adjacency Matrix

For the Adjacency Matrix, we use the rows and columns to represent the same district-grouped station order. Notice that each colored cell marks a direct connection. Color denotes route type, while density denotes travel time. Enjoy!

5. Matrix Design Description

The same district-grouped order appears on both axes; each filled cell records a connection. Red, green, or blue identifies route type, and three density levels classify travel time. Pink strips preserve district membership; the ordering reveals within- and between-district blocks.

6. Network Questions and Findings

  1. Which stations appear central in the network?

    Assessment: Partially. Best view: Node-link. Evidence and finding: Highlighting reveals fourteen maximum-degree stations, each with three neighbors, so no station is uniquely central.

  2. Which districts are strongly connected to one another?

    Assessment: Well. Best view: Adjacency matrix. Evidence and finding: District blocks show 11 East–South links, versus nine Central–North and nine East–West links.

  3. Where are transfer or terminal stations located in the topology?

    Assessment: Well. Best view: Node-link. Evidence and finding: Fill treatments and highlighting place both roles along the chain and at shortcut endpoints, not in one cluster.

  4. Which stations have high passenger volume?

    Assessment: Well. Best view: Node-link. Evidence and finding: Circle area makes Stations 46–50 the largest, with Station 50 highest at 9,850 daily passengers.

  5. Where are the longest direct travel-time connections?

    Assessment: Well. Best view: Both. Evidence and finding: The widest links and darkest cells identify 7–31 (16 minutes), 18–34 (15), and 29–50 (14).

  6. Are particular route types concentrated in particular parts of the network?

    Assessment: Partially. Best view: Adjacency matrix. Evidence and finding: RGB cells show broad dispersal; Metro and Shuttle each have four East–South links, while Express has four in Central–North and East–West.
