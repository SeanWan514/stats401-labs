Temporal Data Visualization with D3

Welcome to Lab7! In this lab, I built an animated commercial network to show how twelve companies and their transaction relationships change across sixty days. Feel free to use the playback controls or select a specific day and hover over to examine connectivity, commercial activity, and cross-regional structure. Enjoy!

1. Dataset

Dataset Description

The two datasets provided contain 12 fabricated companies and 367 undirected commercial transaction records from January 1 through March 1, 2026 (60 days in total). Company records supply id, company name, sector, and region, while each transaction records its date, day, both endpoints, U.S.-dollar amount, type, and count. Notice that data are only used for visualization practice and do NOT represent real companies or transaction histories.

4. Design Description

Design and Interaction Rationale

Notice that for each company, company sector is encoded by node color, region is encoded by node border pattern, and current transaction volume is encoded by node area. These notations together preserve the identity of a company, while letting size reveal which companies are commercially active on the selected day. Link color represents the transaction type, width represents dollar amount ($), and opacity represents the transaction count. Entering relationships fade in and inactive relationships fade out, therefore making the structural change even more explicit.

Also notice that the same company's positions persist across different frames. The force-directed layout is calculated from the complete network once and then reused, so the twelve-node layout stays the same across the time frame, and users can follow the companies more explicitly without relearning the layout every day. Furthermore, Play provides an overview, while Pause, Reset, and the 60-day slider support close comparison. Tooltips provide exact values, and the daily summary reports active companies, active links, total value, and transaction count for the current frame.

5. Temporal-Network Analysis

Questions and Findings

  1. How does overall network connectivity change across the 60 days?

    The overall network becomes more active in the final third of the time frame. Days 1–20 average 5.50 active links and 8.55 active companies, compared with 7.25 links and 9.65 companies on Days 41–60. The daily summary and the number of visible links also make this increase apparent. Day 1 has the minimum of three links, while Day 45, Day 51, and Day 57 each has the maximum of ten links connecting all twelve companies/nodes.

  2. Which companies become more central or commercially active over time?

    Fusion Electronics is the most dominant company through the first forty days (Day 1–Day 40): its node is most often among the largest and most connected, with $1,425,727.51 in incident transaction volume across that period. During Days 41–60, however, Ion Systems and Cedar Retail become more prominent. Their expanding nodes and larger current neighborhoods show this shift, and Ion Systems has the highest late-period transaction volume at $1,096,550.09, followed by Cedar Retail at $901,810.16.

  3. Are there periods when the network consists of relatively separate clusters?

    Yes. The earliest days are especially fragmented. For example, on Day 1, three disconnected pairs are visible and six companies are inactive, while Day 3 and Day 9 each show four separate pairs. Later frames usually contain more active companies, although multiple components remain visible. Notice that stable positions make these separated groups easier to compare from one day to the next.

  4. Which commercial relationships appear, disappear, or become stronger over time?

    Relationships repeatedly enter and leave the network, which is visible through link fade transitions. The Delta Components–Fusion Electronics relationship appears on 20 days and has the largest cumulative value at $440,145.54. The Ion Systems–Lumen Distribution and Apex Textiles–BlueRiver Logistics connections also recur on 20 days, totaling $383,372.36 and $355,196.55 respectively. Ion Systems–Juniper Stores demonstrates a strengthening return: it appears on 10 of the first 20 days, disappears throughout Days 21–40, and returns on 10 of the final 20 days with its average active-day amount increasing from $12,679.95 to $20,189.10.

  5. Does the network become more or less cross-regional over time?

    I think it becomes more cross-regional overall. For each day, I divided cross-regional active links by all active links, then averaged the 20 daily percentages in each period. This produces 55.74% for Days 1–20, 62.98% for Days 21–40, and 72.48% for Days 41–60. As a second check, pooling all link records within each period gives 70/110 (63.64%), 71/112 (63.39%), and 105/145 (72.41%). The methods weight days differently, explaining why another analysis may report different percentages, but both identify the final period as the most cross-regional. Region-specific node borders and link tooltips support this comparison.
