Geospatial Visualization with D3

Two coordinated maps compare the geographic distribution and economic magnitude of the world’s 50 largest economies. Hover, focus, or select a country in either view to identify it in both.

1. Dataset Description

2025 Nominal GDP and World Geography

The provided dataset contains the 50 largest economies by projected 2025 nominal GDP, measured in billions of current U.S. dollars and based on the IMF World Economic Outlook, October 2025. Each GDP record is joined to Natural Earth country geometry by its ISO-3 geographic identifier. Countries outside the supplied top-50 dataset are shown as missing data, never as zero GDP.

2. Choropleth Map

2025 GDP Encoded by Color

Geographic position and country boundaries remain intact. Darker coral-plum indicates greater GDP on a logarithmic color scale, which preserves variation among smaller economies while accommodating the highly skewed values of the United States and China. 3. Dorling Cartogram

Country Area Encodes 2025 GDP

Each top-50 country is represented by a non-overlapping circle positioned near its geographic centroid. Circle area is directly proportional to GDP; approximate location aids recognition, but boundaries, distances, and adjacency are intentionally distorted. This is a Dorling cartogram, not a bubble overlay on the choropleth.

4. Design and Comparison

Geography, Magnitude, and Task Trade-offs

1. The United States and China are most prominent in both views: they receive the darkest choropleth colors and the largest cartogram areas. 2. Compact but wealthy economies such as Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, and South Korea expand substantially in the cartogram, while geographically large Canada, Russia, Australia, and Kazakhstan contract relative to their land area. 3. The choropleth preserves recognizable borders, locations, adjacency, direction, and regional context. 4. The cartogram deliberately sacrifices boundary shape, distance, and exact neighborhood relationships so that area can communicate economic magnitude.

5. The choropleth best supports locating countries, recognizing regional patterns, and relating GDP to geography; its logarithmic color scale prevents the largest two values from suppressing differences among the other economies. 6. The Dorling cartogram best supports comparing magnitude, rank, and economic concentration because circle area is directly proportional to GDP. Linked highlighting restores identity when distortion makes a country difficult to recognize. 7. Changing the principal encoding from color to area makes the extreme concentration of global output much more immediate: the United States and China visually dominate, while lower-ranked economies become small. Color preserves geographic form but encourages broad intensity judgments; area makes quantitative disparity unmistakable while weakening the map’s geographic fidelity. Together, the views expose a useful trade-off rather than declaring either design universally superior.
