3. Cartogram Map

2025 GDP Encoded by Country Area

For the cartogram visualization, the world is continuously reshaped so that the area of each of the 50 matched economies reflects its 2025 GDP. Countries expand or contract and their outlines deform, but shared borders remain connected so neighboring relationships are retained. Missing-data countries keep their original projected-area targets and neutral gray fill rather than receiving GDP-driven sizes. Hover over, focus, or select any country to inspect it in both visualizations. Enjoy!

4. Design and Comparison

Geography, Magnitude, and Task Trade-offs

1. I think the United States and China are most prominent in both views: they receive the darkest choropleth colors (dark purple) and the largest cartogram areas. 2. Geographically compact but wealthy economies such as Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, and South Korea expand substantially in the cartogram, while geographically large Canada, Russia, Australia, and Kazakhstan shrink and become smaller relative to their land area. 3. The choropleth visualization preserves recognizable borders, locations, adjacency, direction, and regional context. 4. The cartogram deliberately distorts boundary shape, relative land area, distance, and direction so that area can communicate economic magnitude, while its shared borders retain neighboring-country relationships.

5. I think the choropleth best supports locating countries and connecting place to GDP because it preserves shapes and borders while color denotes GDP intuitively. Its logarithmic scale prevents the two largest values from suppressing differences among the other economies. 6. I think the contiguous cartogram best supports direct and intuitive GDP-size comparison because deformed country area represents economic magnitude. Placing it next to the choropleth confronts viewers with the contrast between geographic land area and economic size; linked highlighting restores identity despite distortion. 7. Changing the principal encoding from color to area makes the concentration of global output more immediate: the United States and China visually dominate, while lower-ranked economies become small. Color preserves geographic form but encourages broad intensity judgments; area makes quantitative disparity unmistakable while weakening geographic fidelity. Together, the views expose a useful trade-off rather than declaring either design universally superior.
