3. Cartogram Map

2025 GDP Encoded by Country Area

For the cartogram visualization, every country geometry remains present, while the 50 matched economies are resized so that their displayed country area reflects 2025 GDP. Larger economies expand and smaller economies contract; missing-data countries remain visible in neutral gray for geographic context. The non-contiguous layout preserves recognizable country shapes and approximate locations, while intentionally distorting size, distance, and adjacency to emphasize economic magnitude. 4. Design and Comparison

Geography, Magnitude, and Task Trade-offs

1. I think the United States and China are most prominent in both views: they receive the darkest choropleth colors (dark purple) and the largest cartogram areas. 2. Geographically compact but wealthy economies such as Germany, Japan, the United Kingdom, and South Korea expand substantially in the cartogram, while geographically large Canada, Russia, Australia, and Kazakhstan shrink and become smaller relative to their land area. 3. The choropleth visualization preserves recognizable borders, locations, adjacency, direction, and regional context. 4. The cartogram deliberately sacrifices and distorts geographical boundary shape, distance, borders, and exact neighborhood relationships, so that area can communicate economic magnitude.

5. The choropleth best supports locating countries, recognizing regional patterns, and relating GDP to geography; its logarithmic color scale prevents the largest two values from suppressing differences among the other economies. 6. The cartogram best supports comparing magnitude, rank, and economic concentration because the displayed country area is directly proportional to GDP. Linked highlighting restores identity when distortion makes a country difficult to recognize. 7. Changing the principal encoding from color to area makes the extreme concentration of global output much more immediate: the United States and China visually dominate, while lower-ranked economies become small. Color preserves geographic form but encourages broad intensity judgments; area makes quantitative disparity unmistakable while weakening the map’s geographic fidelity. Together, the views expose a useful trade-off rather than declaring either design universally superior.
