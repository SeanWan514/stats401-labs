Design Description

Why These Encodings Work? Two Discussion Questions & Answers

First, both treemaps use the same Python-generated hierarchy strictly as proposed in the lab requirements: World contains Continents, each Continent contains Areas, and each Area contains Countries. I deliberately choose these visual channels because they together can best reflect the embedded information during this visualization. GDP supplies each country's layout weight, therefore larger rectangles indicate greater GDP and the enclosing groups summarize their descendants. A shared categorical palette uses light red for Increase, light yellow for Unchanged, and light green for Decrease. These colors distinguish the supplied GDP-status categories. Solid continent outlines, dashed area outlines, and available group labels clarify the nesting. Country labels identify observations where space permits, while hover and keyboard-focus tooltips always provide the country, continent, area, exact GDP, and status.

Regarding the two treemaps, Squarify favors compact rectangles, generally making relative areas and country labels easier to compare, while Slice-and-dice alternates subdivision direction at successive hierarchy levels, emphasizing nested bands but producing narrow rectangles for some smaller countries. Using identical data, colors, and interactions in both views makes segmentation strategy the principal difference and exposes the trade-off between compact value comparisons and visible hierarchical subdivision.

For me, Squarify is easier to compare, while Slice-and-dice is more intuitive but at the tradeoff of several countries being a bit too narrow to be properly visualized. Therefore, I think the comparison between these two types of treemaps helped me better understand such tradeoffs.
